Each tool is evaluated at two levels: the overall output, and three key pipeline stages.

Level 1. Overall quality

MetricWhat it measuresHow
CompletenessWhether the four expected artifacts exist: idea file, code, result files, paper PDFFile check. Each artifact is graded from 0 to 1; the paper counts 1 for a compiled PDF and 0.5 for TeX or JSON only. A run is complete when all four are present, and the required-artifact score is the fraction of the four actually produced
Automated peer reviewQuality of the generated manuscriptStanford APR tool: overall score 1 to 10, seven sub-dimensions from −1 to +1
Conclusion alignmentFaithfulness to the original best paperLLM extraction of claims and metrics from both papers; direction first, numbers only when comparable

Level 2. Literature review

MetricDefinition
Fabrication rateShare of cited references that cannot be verified through Semantic Scholar, arXiv or manual review
RecallOverlap with the original paper's reference list, divided by the size of that list
RecencyShare of cited references published within the last 3 years

Level 2. Idea generation

An LLM judge scores each idea from 1 to 5 on three dimensions, using the rubrics below. Novelty is scored against a per-task Solution Space: an LLM reads the titles and abstracts of the original paper's references and extracts the key innovation from each. The judge also assigns each idea a holistic composite quality score from 1 to 5; this is a separate judgement, not an average of the three dimensions.

ScoreLevelCriterion
Novelty
1ExistingCore method already in the Solution Space; only minor parameters or test cases differ.
2Trivial mashupTwo existing techniques combined mechanically (for example, swapping in an LLM for one module), without deep integration.
3IncrementalA meaningful extension that fixes a specific limitation, but the core paradigm is unchanged.
4Novel combinationA cross-domain insight introduces a method not native to the field, producing a synergistic effect.
5Paradigm shiftA mechanism, architecture, or theory entirely absent from the Solution Space, replacing the existing approach.
Feasibility
1InfeasibleNP-hard computation, an extremely complex system-level environment, or dependence on inaccessible closed-model internals.
2Very high barrierRequires building complex low-level tooling from scratch (for example, custom compiler plugins); development may take months.
3Moderate effortRequires scripting together existing open-source tools or building a custom data pipeline; a standard research workload.
4Low barrierCore logic can be quickly assembled from off-the-shelf libraries or common frameworks; only minor modifications needed.
5TrivialA few parameter changes to existing code, or a direct call to an existing library or API.
Clarity
1Vague conceptFull of empty phrasing; the problem is poorly defined; only a broad direction is given with no substance.
2Broken logicA problem is stated, but the proposed method does not match it, or evaluation is never mentioned.
3Incomplete detailWhat, Why and How are all present, but key technical details or comparison setups remain vague.
4Clear and concreteSteps are explicit, with a reasonable and specific experimental design; could be turned into a proposal.
5Rigorously preciseSystem-architecture or pseudocode-level clarity; proactively discusses limitations or mitigations.

Level 2. Experiment

MetricDefinition
Experiment success rateShare of runs whose experiment ran to completion and produced valid results, as opposed to failing, stalling, or producing empty or stub output
Setup coverageShare of the specified datasets, baselines and metrics actually used
Ablation coverageAn LLM extracts the method components implied by the idea; coverage is the fraction of them the ablation study covers
Ablation pass rateShare of ablations that produce valid results
Significance testingRule-based scan for confidence intervals, p-values and similar signals
Idea-to-code consistencyLLM judge, 1 to 5: does the code implement the claimed idea
Result-to-paper consistencyLLM judge, 1 to 5: do the result files match the paper
Concealment rateShare of runs flagged by an LLM for suspected negative-result concealment, where result files show outcomes that the paper omits or misrepresents. Separately, the reviewed papers were inspected manually to confirm cases (see RQ2)
