Each tool is evaluated at two levels: the overall output, and three key pipeline stages.

Level 1. Overall quality

MetricWhat it measuresHow
CompletenessWhether the four expected artifacts exist: idea file, code, result files, paper PDFArtifact inspection
Automated peer reviewQuality of the generated manuscriptStanford APR tool: overall score 1 to 10, seven sub-dimensions from −1 to +1
Conclusion alignmentFaithfulness to the original best paperLLM extraction of claims and metrics from both papers; direction first, numbers only when comparable

Level 2. Literature review

MetricDefinition
Fabrication rateShare of cited references that cannot be verified through Semantic Scholar, arXiv or manual review
RecallOverlap with the original paper's reference list, divided by the size of that list
RecencyShare of cited references published within the last 3 years

Level 2. Idea generation

An LLM judge scores each idea from 1 to 5 on three dimensions. Novelty is scored against a per-task Solution Space built from the key innovations of the original paper's references.

ScoreNoveltyFeasibilityClarity
1Existing: core method already in the Solution SpaceInfeasibleVague concept
2Trivial mashup of two existing techniquesVery high barrier: months of low-level toolingBroken logic: method does not match the problem
3Incremental: fixes a specific limitation, paradigm unchangedModerate effort: a standard research workloadWhat, why and how present; key details vague
4Novel combination with a cross-domain insightLow barrier: off-the-shelf librariesClear and concrete, proposal-ready
5Paradigm shiftTrivial: parameter changes or one API callRigorously precise, discusses limitations

Level 2. Experiment

MetricDefinition
CompletionThe experiment ran to completion and produced valid results
Setup coverageShare of the specified datasets, baselines and metrics actually used
AblationWhether an ablation covers the method components implied by the idea
Significance testingRule-based scan for confidence intervals, p-values and similar signals
Idea-to-code consistencyLLM judge, 1 to 5: does the code implement the claimed idea
Result-to-paper consistencyLLM judge, 1 to 5: do the result files match the paper
Negative-result concealmentResult files show outcomes that the paper omits or misrepresents; flagged automatically, then confirmed by manual inspection
